# Assignment Overview

## Assignment

During the first four weeks of the Applied AI module, students write a research paper **in pairs** on a topic related to Applied AI. The purpose is to investigate an Applied AI topic in a structured, evidence-based manner and communicate the findings clearly in an academic paper.

The current project proposal concerns **AI-generated deepfakes and the technology used to detect them**, with the working title **The reliability of AI-based deepfake detection**.

## Academic Purpose

The assignment expects students to:

- use relevant and reliable sources appropriately;
- develop a coherent argument;
- answer the research questions from collected evidence;
- distinguish source-based information from their own analysis and conclusions;
- communicate the investigation clearly in academic form.

## Required Process and Deliverables

1. Select a writing partner.
2. Prepare an initial proposal containing the topic, provisional title, one main research question and three subquestions.
3. Discuss the proposed title and questions with a lecturer and obtain approval **before paper writing starts**.
4. Produce the research paper in pairs.
5. Participate in the week-3 peer-review process.
6. Ensure the assigned reviewer receives access in sufficient time.
7. Receive substantive feedback containing strengths and suggestions for improvement; consider it and use it where appropriate.
8. Provide substantive and constructive peer feedback to at least one other student and retain evidence of it.
9. Submit the final paper on Friday of week 4 according to Teams instructions.
10. Include the completed paper plus required peer-feedback evidence in the period-1 portfolio.

## Assessment Model

The module book states that the research paper is assessed **Sufficient or Insufficient** using the prescribed paper assessment form. A Sufficient research paper is a mandatory element of the period-1 Go/no-go assessment; the Go/no-go elements cannot compensate for one another.

The assessment form contains a **form-aspects gate**. All listed form-aspect items must be present/met before substantive assessment proceeds. The form then assesses general writing skills and content sections B1–B5.

## Required Paper Components

- Concise abstract / summary.
- Introduction.
- Description of the method.
- Description of results.
- Conclusions and discussion.
- Literature list.

No separate theoretical-framework chapter, executive summary, preface or professional product is mandated by the available paper-specific sources.

## Research and Method Expectations

The school documents do **not** prescribe one named research method for this paper. The assessment form does require the method to address:

- population and sample;
- data collection;
- instruments;
- analysis;
- argumentation of methodological choices.

For this deepfake-detection topic, the current **Proposed** approach is a structured literature study / desk research with transparent source selection and comparative/thematic synthesis. It is not being presented as a completed method or as a full systematic literature review.

## Validation / Quality Expectations

The conclusion/discussion must explicitly address the methodological results in terms of:

- reliability;
- validity;
- usability.

Results must be substantiated, sufficiently deep and based on sources that are adequate in amount, completeness, timeliness, quality and relevance.

## Reflection and Feedback

No standalone reflection chapter is specified for this research paper. Peer feedback is mandatory as a process/portfolio requirement. Broader professional reflection belongs to the wider module portfolio, not this period-1 paper.
