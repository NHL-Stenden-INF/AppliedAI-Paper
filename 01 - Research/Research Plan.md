# Research Plan

## Status

**Planned — independently audited. No research findings have been produced.**

## Research Objective

**Proposed:** determine the extent to which current AI-based deepfake detection can be considered reliable within a clearly defined scope by synthesising evidence on detection techniques, reported performance and conditions that reduce effectiveness.

## Research Questions

See [`Research Questions.md`](../00%20-%20Assignment/Research%20Questions.md). Current wording remains **Proposed / lecturer approval To Verify**.

## Question → Method → Evidence → Analysis → Conclusion Route

| Question | Method | Evidence required | Analysis | Future conclusion state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SQ1 | Structured literature study / document analysis | Current detector-technique evidence across final scope | Taxonomic/thematic classification | Defensible technique landscape relevant to MRQ |
| SQ2 | Structured literature study with comparative evidence extraction | Empirical metrics plus dataset/test/generalisation context | Contextual comparison; no unsupported pooling | Bounded performance judgement under defined conditions |
| SQ3 | Structured literature study with robustness/generalisation evidence extraction | Compression, unseen-generator, evasion and other degradation evidence | Thematic + contextual effect synthesis | Evidence-based boundary/failure conditions |
| MRQ | Integrative synthesis of SQ1–SQ3 | Completed SQ evidence and quality assessment | Cross-question synthesis weighted by evidence quality/generalisation | Bounded reliability answer; no universal percentage unless evidence justifies it |

## Phase-2 Entry Conditions

Before evidence extraction is treated as final:

1. confirm or actively seek lecturer approval of the title/MRQ/SQs;
2. define `deepfake`, `current`, `reliably`, `real-world conditions` and final modality coverage;
3. finalise the search/screening/extraction protocol.

Source discovery may begin provisionally while approval is pending, but irreversible scope decisions and final synthesis should wait for approval.

## Planned Research Activities

1. Verify lecturer approval status and record the outcome.
2. Finalise operational definitions and scope boundaries.
3. Define the literature-study protocol: search systems, search strings, dates, publication window, source-type policy, inclusion/exclusion rules, duplicate handling and extraction fields.
4. Run searches and record query/date/result counts.
5. Remove duplicates and perform title/abstract screening.
6. Perform full-text eligibility checks for candidate sources used in major claims.
7. Assess source quality/relevance and label evidence as direct, supporting or contextual.
8. Extract evidence with metric/dataset/condition context preserved.
9. Cross-check high-impact extraction decisions between both authors.
10. Analyse SQ1, SQ2 and SQ3 using the methods defined in `Analysis Framework.md`.
11. Record contradictory/heterogeneous evidence instead of forcing agreement.
12. Re-run the Research Gap Analysis and Evidence Matrix completeness check.
13. Populate the Conclusions Matrix only when a question has sufficient evidence.
14. Determine section-level `Ready for Drafting` status without writing final prose.

## Evidence Coverage Rules

- SQ1 evidence must support a **technique classification**, not merely mention deepfakes.
- SQ2 evidence must include enough context to interpret reported performance.
- SQ3 evidence must directly test or analyse degradation/generalisation/evasion, not merely speculate about it.
- Review papers may map the field, but major performance claims should preferably be traceable to primary empirical evidence.
- Conflicting evidence is retained and analysed.

## Risks and Controls

| Risk | Severity | Why it matters | Control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Three-modality scope too broad | High | B4 requires depth and source completeness within a strict length/time constraint | Early source mapping; narrow only with lecturer approval |
| `Real-world conditions` remains undefined | High | SQ2 becomes inconsistent/unanswerable | Operational definition before extraction |
| Benchmark metrics are incomparable | High | Could produce misleading accuracy claims | Preserve metric/dataset/condition; compare only like with like |
| Evidence is benchmark-only/in-domain | High | Weak basis for real-world reliability | Prioritise cross-dataset/unseen-generator/degradation evidence |
| Literature method is described generically | High | B3 may fail despite good sources | Record population/sample, collection, instruments, analysis and rationale explicitly |
| Fast-moving field makes older evidence stale | Medium | `Current` claim may be unsupported | Define time window; label foundational older sources separately |
| Source quantity substitutes for quality | Medium | Direct B4 source criterion may fail | Source-quality assessment and evidence directness labels |
| Premature writing | High | Unsupported conclusions and revision waste | Maintain drafting gate |
