# Research Plan

## Status

**Planned — Phase 1 foundation only. No research findings are claimed in this file.**

## Research Objective

Proposed objective: evaluate how reliably current AI-based deepfake detection systems distinguish manipulated media from authentic content by synthesising evidence about detection techniques, reported performance and conditions that reduce effectiveness.

## Research Questions

See [`Research Questions.md`](../00%20-%20Assignment/Research%20Questions.md). All current question wording is **Proposed / lecturer approval To Verify**.

## Required Evidence by Question

| Question | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| SQ1 — techniques | Sources describing current AI/ML detection approaches for image, video and audio; features/representations; model families; modality and evaluation context |
| SQ2 — accuracy | Empirical performance evidence with stated datasets/conditions/metrics; evidence under realistic transformations, cross-dataset/unseen-generator or deployment-like conditions where available |
| SQ3 — degrading factors | Empirical or high-quality synthesis evidence concerning compression/quality, new/unseen generators, adversarial/evasion attempts and other factors that reduce effectiveness |
| MRQ | Integrated synthesis from all three SQs, with explicit limits on what `reliability` means and where evidence is comparable |

## Proposed Research Activities

1. **Planned:** confirm lecturer approval of title/MRQ/SQs.
2. **Planned:** operationalise key terms: `deepfake`, `reliably`, `current`, `real-world conditions`.
3. **Planned:** define literature-search and screening protocol.
4. **Planned:** conduct initial search and remove clearly irrelevant/duplicate records.
5. **Planned:** screen titles/abstracts against inclusion/exclusion criteria.
6. **Planned:** assess source quality and relevance.
7. **Planned:** extract comparable evidence into the Evidence Matrix.
8. **Planned:** synthesise SQ1 as a technique taxonomy, SQ2 as contextual performance comparison, and SQ3 as a degradation-factor synthesis.
9. **Planned:** identify evidence conflicts/heterogeneity rather than forcing one accuracy figure.
10. **Planned:** perform a research-completeness check before any final-paper drafting.

## Research Sequence

Approval → operational definitions → search protocol → source discovery → screening → quality assessment → extraction → synthesis by subquestion → gap check → readiness for drafting.

## Risks

| Risk | Impact | Planned control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Topic too broad across three modalities | Insufficient depth within page limit | Early source mapping; narrow only with lecturer agreement if needed |
| Benchmark metrics not comparable | Misleading accuracy synthesis | Preserve dataset, modality, metric and evaluation context; avoid false ranking |
| Rapidly changing generative methods | Evidence becomes outdated | Prioritise current sources and record publication dates |
| Overreliance on benchmark-only results | Weak answer to real-world reliability | Prioritise cross-dataset, degraded, unseen-generator or realistic-condition evidence where available |
| Method under-described for literature research | B3 criterion failure | Explicit population/sample, search, screening, extraction and analysis sections |
| Source quantity crowds out quality | Weak B4 source assessment | Quality/relevance screening and source assessment records |
| Writing begins before evidence is mature | Unsupported conclusions | Maintain phase boundary and drafting readiness gate |

## Completion Criteria for Phase 2

Phase 2 research is complete when:

- the approved questions are stable;
- search/screening procedure is documented;
- each subquestion has sufficient high-quality evidence;
- conflicting evidence is represented, not hidden;
- major claims can be traced to sources;
- the Evidence Matrix has no assessment-critical unexplained gaps;
- the Conclusions Matrix can be populated without speculation;
- the paper outline can be marked Ready for Drafting by section.

Only then should Phase 3 begin.
