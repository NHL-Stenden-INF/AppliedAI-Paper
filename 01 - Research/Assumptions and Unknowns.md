# Assumptions and Unknowns

## Known

- The research-paper assignment occurs during the first four weeks of Applied AI.
- The paper is written in pairs.
- The proposal must contain a topic, provisional title, one main research question and three subquestions.
- The title and questions require lecturer discussion/approval before writing.
- Peer review occurs in week 3.
- Final submission is Friday of week 4.
- The paper is a mandatory Sufficient/Insufficient element of the period-1 Go/no-go.
- The assessment form requires an abstract, introduction, method, results, conclusions/discussion and literature list.
- The form-aspects gate includes 8–10-page volume, layout, structure, figures/tables, spelling/grammar and APA.
- The current project topic/title/questions concern reliability of AI-based deepfake detection across image, video and audio.

## Inferred

- The current subject sheet is a proposal/registration artefact rather than evidence of lecturer approval.
- A structured literature review is suitable for the current questions and can satisfy the B3 method rubric if transparently executed.
- The practical target audience is likely lecturers/examiners and technically literate IT readers, but the assessment form does not name it.

## Assumptions

- No primary empirical study is mandatory unless additional Teams/lecturer instructions say otherwise.
- The authors can use a literature-based source population/sample to satisfy the method rubric.
- The broad three-modality scope can remain feasible only if the synthesis is tightly structured; this must be tested early in Phase 2.

- Whether the abstract is counted in the 8–10-page volume rule.
- Exact intended target audience.
- Exact publication time window implied by `currently`.
- Exact metric/threshold that will operationalise `reliable`.
