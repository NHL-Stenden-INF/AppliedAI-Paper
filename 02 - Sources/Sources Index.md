## Source-quality rules

Evidence quality and evidence directness are assessed separately.

For every major technical claim, check:

- direct relevance to the research question;
- publication date and role of older/foundational work;
- publication type and peer-review/official-authority status;
- publisher, venue or institution;
- method/evidence transparency;
- dataset, metric and test condition;
- DOI or official/stable URL;
- stated limitations and generalisation boundary.

Review papers may map the field, but important performance claims must retain their empirical context. Preprints and government sources are limited to the role they can support.
